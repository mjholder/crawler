## What I worked on (second session)

Fixed wave-transition timing and unified all entity animations under `AnimationPlayer`.

Split the weapon attack sprite into `wind_up` and `swing` pieces and re-timed damage to match the visual hit.

**Wave transition gating:** Between-wave transitions were advancing immediately on enemy death, cutting off the death animation. Added a `death_finished` signal to `Enemy` with a virtual `_death_is_immediate()` hook (returns `true` in base class). `Skeleton` overrides to `false` and calls `_emit_death_finished()` from its `_on_anim_player_finished` handler when the `death` clip ends. `CombatEvent._on_enemy_died` now awaits all pending `death_finished` signals before calling `_advance_phase()`.

**AnimationPlayer as sole animation driver:** `AnimatedSprite2D` is now a passive renderer — it holds sprite frame data but never calls `play()` from script. `AnimationPlayer` method tracks call `Sprite.play("frame_name")` at specific timestamps. This gives full control over timing, sequencing (e.g. skeleton `windup` at 0s then `swing` at 0.4s within one `attack` clip), and future effects (hitstop, post-death pause, modulate flash, screen shake). Scripts reduced to one `_anim_player.play()` call and one `animation_finished` handler per entity.

**Fixed `.tscn` dependency cascade:** Rewriting `weapon.tscn` and `equipment.tscn` accidentally placed `[sub_resource]` blocks after the root `[node]` declaration. Godot 4's parser requires all sub_resources before any nodes — violating this produces silent "missing dependencies" errors that cascade through `battle_axe.tres` → `debug_shop.tres` → `world_map.tscn`. Rewrote both files with correct ordering and corrected the attack clip length from 0.2s to 0.4s (2 frames ÷ speed 5.0).
